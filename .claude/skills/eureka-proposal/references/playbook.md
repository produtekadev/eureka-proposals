# Playbook: what has worked

From accepted proposals (Nickel AI, Ofiuko, Rojo Estudio Creativo, SISU, Trolley.ai). Use it to shape scope and price, not as a script.

## Structure that closes

Page order: Cover, Context, Goal, Proposal, Team, Start. Keep it minimal and put the important things first.

0. **Minimal cover.** The goal and "Duration & price" first; who, prepared by and dates in one small line below (`cover.goal`).
1. **Context in their words.** Who they are (size, team, tools), 3 pains with evidence, a quote, why now ("the opportunity"). Clients accepted when they recognized themselves in this section. An illustration of the problem (e.g. `too-many-apps`) goes here, between the pains title and the pain cards.
2. **A Goal, not a feature list.** Call the section "Goal" (`focus.nav`). Federico's proposal worked because it picked one North Star (the founder's cockpit) instead of promising all 12 departments at once. Show what the business looks like after working with us: one statement, benefit cards with icons (`focus.benefits`), and one Notion AI chat window (`focus.mockups` with `type: "chat"`): their real question, the answer with its sources, 2 or 3 more example questions, and what Notion reads from (Drive, meetings, product catalog...). One box is enough; three windows felt like too much. The proposal section then explains how we get there.
3. **Phases with separate prices.** Every accepted proposal split the work into phases or modules, each with its own price. Discovery or audit first, then build, then optional layers.
4. **One fixed price, with tags.** Every phase is included in the total. Tags still say what the project needs (`essential`), what we advise (`recommended`) and what could wait (`optional`). Clients can't remove phases on the page; if they ask to change the scope, we edit the proposal and republish it.
5. **Included / not included.** Always both. The "not included" list prevents scope creep later (licenses, external automations, extra modules, ongoing support).
6. **Guarantee.** 1 month (Rojo) to 3 months (SISU) of fixes within the delivered scope.
7. **Team and clients.** The people assigned, then the client logo grid.
   Illustrations (`template/assets/illustrations/`): `map` for the audit or discovery phase, `build` for the build phase, `scale` for training and launch, `team-work` next to the closing, `too-many-apps` in Context. Phase drawings sit on the left of the ticks, small, with white space around them.
   Totals box: only Total and Duration. The 50/50 split goes in the text below, not as a third figure.
8. **One clear call to action.** Accept on the page, then deposit by bank transfer, then book the kickoff.

## Pricing references (USD)

Standard prices since 2026-11-23: **audit or discovery USD 500**, **training USD 300**. The build phase carries the rest of the scope and the price difference.

| Piece | Typical price | Duration | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workspace audit and report | 1,500 | 2 to 3 weeks | Nickel AI |
| Discovery and roadmap (large org) | 1,500 | 2 weeks | Trolley.ai Phase 1 |
| Core implementation (small team, one operating system) | 1,500 | 3 to 5 weeks | Ofiuko, SISU, Rojo |
| Implementation bundle after an audit | 1,000 | after report | Nickel AI |
| Notion AI setup (agents, prompt library, training) | 300 | in parallel | SISU (optional) |
| Data migration from Drive or Sheets | 500 | about 1 week | Ofiuko (optional) |
| Monthly build for large orgs (2 departments a month) | 4,000 to 6,000 a month, 5,000 average | monthly | Trolley.ai Phase 2 |

- Small teams (under 15 people): total usually USD 1,200 to 2,500.
- Credit what the client already paid for earlier work (Rojo: USD 300 discounted).
- As Notion Consulting Partners we can offer months of Notion AI Business at no cost (Rojo: 6 months, SISU: 3 months). Mention it as a bonus when it applies; confirm with the user first, and check the client didn't already get it in an expert session. When it applies, show it near the price (it offsets it), say it is on a new workspace, and don't bury it in "Included".
- If asked for hours, give an average with a range (Trolley.ai: 125 hours a month on average, 100 to 150).

## Payment and terms

- Default: 50% when they accept, 50% on delivery. Monthly builds: paid at the start of each month. Discovery for big projects: 100% upfront.
- Bank transfer. No card payments by default (no commissions).
- Valid for about two weeks. Projected start: the week after acceptance.
- NDA: offer it when the client handles sensitive data (health, finance).

## Wording that worked

- Section titles: short, two lines, second line in italics (`What we<br><em>heard.</em>`, `What we'll build<br><em>and what it costs.</em>`).
- Proposal section title: use `What we'll build<br><em>and what it costs.</em>` (Spanish: `Lo que vamos a construir<br><em>y lo que cuesta.</em>`). The team prefers it to "N phases, clear prices", which sounds generated.
- Pains: a bold short name, one line in their words, one line of consequence.
- "Opportunity": why now (an upcoming hire, a growth moment, a failed tool), in 2 sentences.
- Closing: "We're ready to build *your Eureka moment.*" / "Listos para construir *tu momento Eureka.*"

## Wording that confused clients (avoid)

- Deliverable titles must make sense without their detail. "Optimized operating guide" didn't; "Your AI tools connected" (Notion connected to Claude, ChatGPT or Grok) does.
- Call a product knowledge base a "Product catalog and specs", not a "Products database" (it describes products, it isn't inventory).
- Don't promise "no AI credits": Notion agents use credits. Say "one plan, everything included" (no paying separately for CRM, work management and AI).
- Not included, be precise: "Additional custom AI agents" (Slack can be connected), "Moving media files out of Google Drive" (key files can be linked or moved).
- Pains: never single out a client's team member ("when Katie is out"); describe the situation instead.
